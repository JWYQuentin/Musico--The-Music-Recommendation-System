Musico: a two-stage music recommender

By Zunhong (Quentin) Mei · code and full write-up on GitHub

A two-tower neural network picks 1,000 candidate tracks for each listener and a gradient-boosted ranker reorders the best 500. It was trained on 13 months of Last.fm listening and scored on the two weeks that followed.

Every number on this page is a total over thousands of listeners, read from the project's reports. The page shows no track titles, artists or individual listeners: titles and artists come from a dataset that is shared for research only.

How the models compare

A model is scored on the tracks each listener plays for the first time in the scoring period. Recall@K is the share of those tracks in the model's top K; NDCG@K also rewards placing them near the top. Every model is fitted on the same data.

Metric K

What each stage adds

The gain from each step, with a 95% interval from resampling listeners. An interval that stays clear of zero is a real gain on this data.

At K = 500 the rankers only reorder the same 500 candidates, so recall there cannot change.

What the ranker relies on

Mean absolute SHAP value per feature over the ranker's candidates: how far each feature moves a candidate's score, on average. What the listener has already played by the same artist matters most.

Listeners it has never seen

One listener in ten was left out of training. They are served from the listens they hand in, cut to their N most recent tracks, and compared with the same listeners scored with vectors learned in training.

From a short form instead of listens

The same listeners served from answers they could give in a sign-up form: favourite genres, how mainstream they are, favourite artists. The answers are read off their own listening, so these are upper bounds. Genre answers go through retrieval only; artists and listens go through both stages.

Tracks nobody has played

A version of the retrieval model reads only a track's audio, lyrics and genres, so it can place a track with no listens. Here some tracks were removed from training entirely, and models are scored as if those were the only tracks in the catalogue.

Metric

Who the system serves

Measured on each listener's top 10 in the period. Tracks are cut into head, middle and tail so that each third holds a third of all training listening. A model is fair to a group when its share of slots is close to the group's share of what listeners went on to discover.

The popularity penalty and its price

Marking popular candidates down at ranking time moves slots towards the tail, and every step costs accuracy. Its strength λ was picked on val by a rule fixed in advance: the largest that costs under 2% of NDCG@10.

Serving it

The models run as a local API. For every known listener it gives the same top 10 as the offline pipeline. For someone new it folds a vector in from the listens they hand in. Times are for one request on one machine, measured over requests.

Limits

  • Offline evaluation only. A listener playing a track is taken as wanting it: there are no skips, likes or play lengths, and no way to test what a listener would have done with a different list.
  • Two weeks of test, one dataset. Settings were picked on val, where gains were about twice what test confirmed, so every claim is checked on test with an interval.
  • Bias is measured against what listeners went on to play, which was itself shaped by whatever was recommending music to them at the time.
